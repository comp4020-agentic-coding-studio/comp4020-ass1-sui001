Every scale of thought, one scroll away

Everything responds to something, eventually. Scroll down and watch the response times fall off a cliff — from continents to your own twitching finger to a CPU that doesn't even know what waiting means.

  1. Deep time

    ~13.8 billion years

    The universe's one and only reaction so far: expanding. Ask it to hurry up and it will, technically, in another few billion years.

  2. Galactic

    ~230 million years

    One trip around the galactic centre. The sun has done this about twenty times since it formed, which is either a lot or nothing, depending on how it's feeling.

  3. Geological

    ~5 million years

    A mountain range rises about as fast as your fingernails grow. Erosion is patient. Continents are patient. You are not.

  4. Slow plant life

    ~hours to days

    A sunflower turning to face the sun, a vine finding a trellis — most plants do react, they just refuse to be rushed about it.

  5. Slow animals

    ~1–5 seconds

    A sloth deciding to move. A starfish deciding anything. Glacial by animal standards, geological by yours.

  6. You

    Play the test above to find out.

    Somewhere between the sloth and the flytrap, probably. Go click some dots and we'll place you properly.

  7. Venus flytrap

    ~100 milliseconds

    A confirmed, measured 100ms to snap shut on a trigger-hair touch — not muscle, a mechanical buckling instability in the leaf itself. Forterre et al., Nature (2005).

  8. Fast animals

    ~0.13–2.7 milliseconds

    A mantis shrimp's punch: 2.7ms, faster than a bullet leaving a barrel (UC Berkeley, 2004). A trap-jaw ant's bite: 0.13ms, the fastest measured animal movement there is.

  9. Computing

    ~0.0000003 milliseconds

    One clock cycle on a 3GHz CPU. It has already finished before your eye has finished blinking, several thousand times over.
